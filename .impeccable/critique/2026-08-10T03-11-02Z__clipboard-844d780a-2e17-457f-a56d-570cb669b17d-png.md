## Design Specificity Verdict

LLM assessment: the parchment-and-pine palette is committed, distinctive, and emotionally right for a calm privacy-first companion, with hand-checked AA contrast on pills, muted text, and the CTA. But the skeleton underneath is a category-generic setup wizard: eyebrow, H1, card, icon-tile rows with status pills, success alert, primary button, disclosure. Swap the copy and this ships unchanged as printer onboarding or a CI runner status page. Nothing in the visual language says "voice" or "companion": no waveform motif, no presence, no preview of the thing being set up.

Deterministic scan: CLI detect.mjs on Readiness.tsx and on the readiness directory returned zero findings. The in-browser scan on the live DOM (fake-services dev server) found 2 to 4 hits: gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow on the card (1px border plus 48px blur shadow), cream-palette on the page background, and line-length x2 on the disclosure step's paragraphs. The detector caught what the design review missed: the hairline-border-plus-wide-soft-shadow card treatment is a generic AI-tell finish that quietly undercuts an otherwise distinctive palette. cream-palette is a false positive here: the warm paper background is the product's deliberate, consistently applied identity with verified contrast, not an unexamined default. line-length is marginal (about 86 vs 80 chars) on one-time compliance copy in the previous step, not a defect of the readiness surface.

Visual overlays: overlay injection succeeded in the assessment browser session (4 overlay nodes plus banner on the disclosure view, banner plus card label on the readiness view). Note: the app's CSP (script-src 'self') blocked the standard script-tag injection of detect.js, so the same source was injected via CDP eval as fallback; no persistent [Human] tab remains after the run.

## Overall Impression

A trustworthy, well-hardened surface whose moment of truth falls flat. When all three capabilities go green, the screen delivers a diagnostic report instead of a companion: six inert text lines, two of them instructions to do nothing, and the one reassurance that matters ("Capture is stopped until the session starts") buried in log-speak inside a bordered box. The single biggest opportunity is to distill the ready state into one human moment and let the CTA own the page.

## What's Working

- Distinctive, calm palette (parchment #f7f4ec, pine #173c30/#226e52) with verified AA contrast: Ready pill about 5.7:1, muted text about 5.9:1, CTA about 6.1:1. No default-SaaS tells in the color story.
- Privacy reassurance is specific and honest ("Raw audio and your full local history are not sent", "Capture is stopped until the session starts"), exactly the fear this product must address, and states are hardened: loading spinners, error alert, transcript-only fallback, 2s polling that self-heals stale mic status, prefers-reduced-motion respected.
- Real accessibility scaffolding: semantic ul/li capability list, aria-labelledby cards, role=status, aria-expanded on the disclosure, 3px focus rings, 44px primary target.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Noise rows dilute the ready state.** "Next: No action needed.", "Next: None." and the reason lines add six lines carrying zero information when everything is green, pushing the CTA down and burying the one reassurance that matters. Empty "Next:" lines train users to stop reading, so the real warning later gets skipped. Fix: when a capability is ready, render icon plus label plus pill only; suppress the Next line entirely when there is no action; keep reason and Next for needs_action and unavailable. Suggested command: $impeccable distill
2. **[P2] Implementation jargon in user-facing copy.** "Local readiness", "Audio sidecar: ready. Session capability: issued in memory.", "Selected Nemotron and Kokoro runtime is ready." (also grammatically off). First-time users cannot tell diagnostics from problems, and in a privacy product jargon reads as risk. Fix: rewrite in human voice ("Your local audio engine is running.", "Everything stays on this device."); keep raw diagnostics but label them as such inside Details. Suggested command: $impeccable clarify
3. **[P2] Flat emotional peak.** The all-ready moment looks like a CI status page, not a companion about to listen: three identical rows, no greeting, no voice preview, no sense of a presence waiting, and the journey ends on a utilitarian left-aligned button plus a developer-facing disclosure. Fix: add one warm line in the companion's voice under the subhead (e.g. "I'm ready when you are.") and an optional "hear a sample" affordance on the Voice output row that plays a 1s Kokoro clip. Suggested command: $impeccable delight
4. **[P2] Privacy control gap plus a11y dangling reference.** After acknowledgment the disclosure text is unreachable (localStorage remember, no link back), and the closed Details button carries aria-controls pointing at an id that is not rendered. A privacy-first product should let users re-read the boundary at the moment they press Start. Fix: add a quiet "Privacy terms" link beside Details that re-opens the disclosure content in place; always render the region (hidden attribute) or drop aria-controls when closed. Suggested command: $impeccable harden
5. **[P3] Collision and dead zone at the decision point.** The success callout and "Start session" button have zero gap, and the left-aligned button floats in a 46rem card with a dead zone to its right, so reassurance and action blur into one blob and the CTA loses gravitational pull. Fix: give the action row margin-top 1rem to match the note rhythm; make the CTA full-width on mobile and right-weighted under the callout on desktop. Suggested command: $impeccable layout

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer)**: "LOCAL READINESS" eyebrow is unexplained jargon; the subhead "Secure local connection established. Audio capture has not started." reads as a contradiction (established but not started?); "Next: No action needed." and "Next: None." are instructions to do nothing, which confuse more than reassure; "Nemotron" and "Kokoro" appear with no gloss; opening Details surfaces "Session capability: issued in memory." which sounds like an error; two headings ("Local readiness" and "Readiness") say the same thing.

**Casey (distracted mobile user)**: the "Start session" button touches the callout border (no gap), inviting mis-taps; the card is tall and the CTA sits mid-scroll with a large dead zone to its right, so the eye has no anchor; three rows of small muted text compete with the one decision; the 36px "Details" ghost toggle is the second tappable thing on screen and visually rhymes with the CTA area.

**Sam (screen reader / keyboard user)**: the closed Details button's aria-controls references a region that does not exist in the DOM; returning users get checkReadiness auto-run on mount, so the page silently swaps from disclosure card to readiness card under a focus point that never moved; role="status" on the subhead announces "Secure local connection established. Audio capture has not started." as one burst with no heading context; capability reasons and Next lines read as undifferentiated paragraphs with no cue that they are inert.

## Minor Observations

- The card's 1px border plus 48px-blur shadow (detector: gpt-thin-border-wide-shadow) is the one generic finish on an otherwise committed surface; a flatter, more intentional card treatment would match the palette's confidence.
- Disclosure-step paragraphs run about 86 chars per line against an 80 target; marginal on one-time compliance copy.
- The chevron's 0.15s rotation is the only motion on the screen; state changes (needs_action to ready) swap with no feedback at all.
- Error alerts render below the card in main flow; on a phone with the keyboard up, "Microphone access was not granted." can land outside the viewport with no focus move.
- The icon tiles (muted beige squares) are the strongest visual element on the card yet carry the least information; the pills, which carry the actual verdict, are the quietest element.
- Warning/destructive badge contrast (about 4.9:1 at 12px medium) passes AA with little headroom; worth a token bump if the type gets any smaller.
- The disclosure step's three outbound links are the only place privacy documentation lives; the readiness screen inherits none of it.
- The row-level "Next: ..." pattern forces a sentence even when the answer is null, which is how "Next: None." was born.

## Questions to Consider

- If all three rows say Ready, what does the list still earn that a single "Everything is ready on this device" moment would not?
- Is this screen reassuring the human, or reporting to the engineer; and who exactly is "Session capability: issued in memory" for?
- What would a companion do here that a setup wizard would not: greet you, breathe, let you hear its voice before you commit?
- If the privacy boundary is the product's whole promise, why is it the only thing you cannot re-read at the moment you press Start?
